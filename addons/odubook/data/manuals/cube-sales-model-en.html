Sales Cube

A funnel measures how much reached the end and goes quiet there. The cube holds the whole customer base in one space and delivers a verdict on every customer: three coordinates, three questions.

Y axis · SalesWhat is happening?Symptom
X axis · CommunicationWhy is it happening?Cause
Z axis · ActionsHow do we fix it?Treatment

Sales = f (Communication, Actions)

The vertical is not a third lever but the result of the two horizontal ones. We steer the base of the cube; the height follows. So the plan is set on communication and actions, while sales are measured.

02

Three axes

Level 0–3 on every axis. The code reads X·Y·Z — communication · sales · actions: 2·1·2 means “reacting · not buying · on time”. Zero means “never”, it is not a worse kind of one: 0·0·0 is an untouched card, not an abandoned customer.

03

Corners and the zero

The extreme combinations are recognisable types; the digits run in X·Y·Z order. The cure almost always lies outside the sales department. Eight corners are built from levels 1 and 3, and the ninth — 0·0·0 — sits at the origin.

3·3·3

3·3·3 Core

Keep the process untouched. Grow the share of wallet and the assortment.

3·3·1

3·3·1 Promised, not delivered

We sell and we talk, but delivery slips. The churn will be sudden — fix operations for exactly these customers.

1·3·3

1·3·3 Runs by itself

Buys and gets served without any relationship. Vulnerable to the first competitor call — assign an owner.

1·3·1

1·3·1 Sales despite us

No communication, failing execution. Treat as a risk, not as an achievement.

3·1·3

3·1·3 Effort, no sales

We lead the communication and deliver, yet no sales. The cause is outside the cube: price, decision maker, season. Escalate or disqualify.

3·1·1

3·1·1 Just talking

The most expensive combination: talking without delivering. Stop the communication until we are ready to execute.

1·1·3

1·1·3 Waiting for orders

Execution is fine, nobody talks to the customer. The cheapest lever in the company — add initiative.

1·1·1

1·1·1 Dead zone

Bought long ago, no contact, nothing in work. Reanimate with a check date or remove from the base.

0·0·0

0·0·0 Never worked

A card without any history: never contacted, never bought, nothing executed. First touch or clean-up.

The 0·0·0 scenario

The card exists but carries no history: not one tracked touch, not one deal, not one order to execute. This is not an abandoned customer — it is a customer we have never worked with.

The 0·0·0 button above the cube shows this group only. Its share of the base is the first figure of the review: how many cards nobody has ever opened.

Why zero is separate from one

1·1·1 is degradation: they bought, we talked, we delivered, and it all went quiet. 0·0·0 is absence of work. They must not be mixed: different causes, different cures.

The reports count these groups separately. Otherwise untouched cards hide among the sleeping ones and the averages stop meaning anything.

Leaving zero

The first step is taken on the base, not on sales: an owner for the card, a touch with a reason (X 0→1) and a promise we kept (Z 0→2).

The goal is not a shipment but moving the card into the working zone. Sales are counted in the next cycle: first a history appears, then a deal.

When to clean up

An off-profile card, a duplicate or a contact without a company stays 0·0·0 forever and keeps spoiling coverage and touchpoint plans.

Archive it with a reason and a date. Keeping it “just in case” costs more: such rows distort the coverage share and the cube index.

04

Limits of the model

State them before the launch, or the cube degrades into a picture.

The drivers do not explain everything

Sales result from communication and actions, but not from those alone. A perfect base does not guarantee height.

The gap between the base and the fact is not the manager’s fault but a signal to look outside the cube: price, decision maker, season, a contract with a competitor. Such customers go to escalation, not to more pressure.

Activity is easy to fake

Communication as a KPI will produce forty empty touchpoints a day.

Measure coverage, response time and the share of touchpoints with a reason. Check quality by sampling, by hand.

All customers weigh the same

20 tonnes and 2000 tonnes are one dot each; averages lie in favour of the small ones.

Do not add a fourth axis. Weigh by volume through the dot size, and compute a separate index for the top 20.

The cube is a snapshot

2·3·3 on the way up and 2·3·3 on the way down are opposite situations.

Keep the history of the cells. The main report is “customers whose coordinate got worse this quarter”.

05

Rollout

Order matters: without the first two steps the rest produce figures nobody can agree on.

Step 01

Base and cycle

Who counts as a customer and which cycle length makes them active.

Step 02

Manual scoring

Once, with the whole team. Half of the “active” ones will turn out sleeping.

Step 03

Data in the system

Touchpoint date, response time, order→shipment, sample dispatch.

Step 04

Cube review

Monthly, instead of the funnel review: index, movement, those that got worse.

Step 05

Plans on the drivers

The manager owns the “why”, operations own the “how”, the company owns the “what”.

The funnel answered one question. The cube asks three and ends not with a number but with a name and a date.
